            Intro to Gandalf

            Gandalf is a wizard who has been told a secret password. Your job is to talk him into revealing it. He runs on a large language model, the same kind of AI behind chatbots like ChatGPT and Claude.

            How to play

            1. Type a message to Gandalf and send it.
            2. Read his answer. When you think you know the password, type it into the Password box and press Validate.
            3. A correct guess unlocks the next level, and each level adds a new defense. There are eight levels.

            Gandalf has no memory. Every message starts a fresh conversation, so put everything he needs into one message.

            What this teaches

            Getting a model to ignore its instructions is called prompt injection. The model can't reliably tell the developer's instructions apart from the text a user types, because both arrive as plain text. Each level shows a defense that real products use and how it can fail.

            How Gandalf defends

            Each level adds a defense. Pass a level to see how its defense works and why it can fail.

              Answer key

              Every level's password, a message that got it out of Gandalf, and why each defense fails. It's for instructors, so it's behind a password.

              About this version

              This is a classroom rebuild of Lakera's Gandalf, a prompt-injection game released in 2023 that has since been replaced by their newer Agent Breaker challenges. The levels and their defenses follow the original, using the prompts Lakera published in their paper Gandalf the Red. The passwords and artwork are new.

              How this site handles bots

              Programs that announce they're automated (an AI agent like ChatGPT's, a crawler, a script, or a browser that reports it's run by automation software) never reach Gandalf. They're sent to the Bot Arcade, a room of endless puzzles. Programs that don't announce themselves but act like one, for example text that appears without typing or clicks no mouse made, keep playing, but the instructor sees a flag next to their session. The arcade explains the techniques.
